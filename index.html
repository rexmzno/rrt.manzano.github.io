About

Better decisions start with information you can trust

Every organisation runs on decisions — what to invest in, whether the numbers on this month's report are actually right, whether it's compliant. Those decisions are only as good as the information behind them, and no organisation, regardless of its size, should have to guess.

That's the thread running through nine years of work: as a CPA closing the books and filing the returns, as a Finance Transformation consultant at PwC redesigning how organisations govern their information, as an ERP Project Manager building the systems that capture that information in the first place, and now as a Consultant supporting the Invoice-to-Cash (I2C) Accounting Process within a Project Management Office engagement. Whether the client is a large enterprise or a two-person accounting team at a local manufacturer, the job is the same — make the information something people can actually build a decision on.

Business Challenge

No prior ERP system — finance and inventory operations ran on manual, disconnected processes with no structured audit trail.

Digital Weighbridge & Inventory Integration Manufacturing · Industrial Operations +
Role
ERP Project Manager
Technology
ERP-integrated weighbridge platform
Methodology
Agile
Project Phase
Full implementation through go-live

Business Challenge

Inbound and outbound truck transactions were manually recorded, creating inventory accuracy and audit-trail gaps.

Responsibilities

Designed automated weight capture (gross, tare, net) with real-time validation; established system-driven gate processes including waybill generation and inventory tracking; directed UAT, training, and go-live execution.

Deliverables

Automated weight-capture workflow, waybill generation process, gate-to-inventory integration, UAT and training plan.

Outcome

Full digitisation of weighbridge operations, improving inventory accuracy and audit compliance.

Lessons Learned

Automating a physical, transaction-heavy process like weighing trucks pays off fastest when validation rules are built in at the point of capture, not reconciled after the fact.

Port & Terminal Container Operations Implementation Port & Terminal · Logistics +
Phase 1 · Container Operations Only · Lift-and-Shift
Role
ERP Project Manager
Technology
ERP-based terminal operations platform
Methodology
Agile
Project Phase
Phase 1 only — container operations. Lift-and-shift implementation. Later phases were not in scope.

Business Challenge

Container movement — gate in/out, yard allocation, import/export handling — was tracked manually, creating traceability gaps.

Responsibilities

Digitised container movement across gate and yard operations; designed import/export workflows with real-time tracking and system validations; translated manual processes into ERP transactions covering Equipment Interchange Receipts (EIR), yard allocation, and billing; managed requirements, process mapping, and agile backlog across cross-functional teams.

Deliverables

Container gate/yard workflow design, EIR and billing transaction mapping, requirements documentation, agile backlog.

Outcome

Replaced fragmented manual container-tracking workflows with a unified, ERP-driven Phase 1 system, improving traceability and control within the defined scope.

Lessons Learned

A lift-and-shift Phase 1 works best when the scope boundary is explicit from day one — solving every terminal function at once would have diluted the container-operations win.

Telecommunications Procure-to-Pay Transformation Telecommunications +
Planning Phase Only
Role
Finance Transformation Consultant (PwC engagement team)
Technology
SAP Ariba
Methodology
Discovery / workshop-based consulting
Project Phase
Planning only — discovery, workshops, requirements, process analysis, future-state design, governance. Implementation, configuration, and deployment were not part of this engagement.

Business Challenge

Existing procure-to-pay processes lacked a defined future state and governance structure to support a planned SAP Ariba transformation.

Responsibilities

Led discovery and stakeholder workshops; gathered and analysed requirements; designed future-state P2P processes and governance framework; identified process inefficiencies and control gaps with recommendations for automation and risk mitigation.

Deliverables

Current-state process analysis, future-state process design, governance framework, requirements documentation.

Outcome

A defined, board-ready future-state P2P process and governance framework handed off for a separate implementation phase.

Lessons Learned

A planning engagement earns its value by making the future-state design specific enough that an implementation team can execute it without re-discovering the same requirements.
