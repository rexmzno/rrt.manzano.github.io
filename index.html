About

Better decisions start with information you can trust

Every organisation runs on decisions — what to invest in, whether the numbers on this month's report are actually right, whether it's compliant. Those decisions are only as good as the information behind them, and no organisation, regardless of its size, should have to guess.

That's the thread running through nine years of work: as a CPA closing the books and filing the returns, as a Finance Transformation consultant at PwC redesigning how organisations govern their information, as an ERP Project Manager building the systems that capture that information in the first place, and now as a Consultant supporting the Invoice-to-Cash (I2C) Accounting Process within a Project Management Office engagement. Whether the client is a large enterprise or a two-person accounting team at a local manufacturer, the job is the same — make the information something people can actually build a decision on.

Business Challenge

Truck movements depended on manual recording and disconnected operational processes — inconsistent truck tracking, duplicated records, manual weight computation, paper-based documentation, and a difficult audit trail, with no centralised way to track a truck from gate entry to exit.

Lessons Learned

A weighbridge system isn't just about capturing a weight — the process design work (what state is a truck in, and who needs to know) mattered as much as the weight-capture logic itself.

Business Challenge

Operational and financial information was spread across Sage, Progress Plus, engineering data, and manual reporting — with job costing, quoting, investment decisions, and monthly reporting operating independently. Responsibilities

Lessons Learned

The hardest part of building decision-support tools isn't the modelling — it's designing them so every output ends with a recommended action, not just a number, which is what actually changes how management uses them.
